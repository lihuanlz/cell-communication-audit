## Abstract

Cells sense and encode information with biochemical circuits, yet what is inferable about a circuit from measurable outputs remains unsystematically characterized. Here we transfer an identifiability audit—developed for bioanalytical assays, where digital counting proved calibration-free—to cellular information circuits. Auditing the phosphorylation–dephosphorylation cycle and the p53 pulse generator, we derive their degeneracy groups, quantify the Fisher barrier separating identifiable structure from molecular scale, and establish one-sided reporting protocols. For p53, pulse observables themselves select circuit topology: uniform-amplitude, dose-counting pulses require excitable architectures with positive feedback, excluding pure negative-feedback models. Across both circuits, the calibration-free regime is one mathematical limit—when analog scale factors cancel, information survives only in counting statistics—corroborated in three published single-cell datasets. Blind pre-registered adjudications bound this limit empirically: falsified for Msn2 amplitude stratification; sufficient for NF-κB spatial and low-dose order, but not at dose saturation or along stimulus duration. Cells, like assay engineers, count when they cannot calibrate.

## 1. Introduction

Cells are information processors. They sense extracellular and intracellular signals through biochemical circuits and encode the results in the dynamics of their molecular states [Tkačik & ten Wolde, 2025]. Two circuit modes implement the two fundamental operations. Static sensors—exemplified by the phosphorylation–dephosphorylation cycle (PdPC), the covalent-modification motif at the heart of kinase signaling [Goldbeter & Koshland, 1981]—convert an input level into an output level through a steady-state input–output relation. Dynamic encoders—exemplified by the p53 tumor suppressor, which responds to DNA damage with discrete, stereotyped pulses whose number reports dose [Lahav, 2004; Batchelor, 2008]—convert an input level into a temporal pattern. A mature information-theoretic literature quantifies how much information such circuits transmit [Tostevin & ten Wolde, 2009; Govern & ten Wolde, 2014; Selimkhanov, 2014; Bowsher & Swain, 2014], and a mature phenomenological literature catalogues their dynamics [Geva-Zatorsky, 2006; Purvis, 2012].

A prior question has received far less systematic attention: **what can be inferred about the circuit itself from what experiments can measure?** The kinetic parameters of cellular circuits are inferred from output statistics—dose–response curves, pulse periods, amplitudes, counts—yet it is now well documented that very different parameter sets can produce nearly identical circuit behavior [Prinz, 2004; Goaillard & Marder, 2021]. This degeneracy is usually reported as an empirical observation about specific models. Structural identifiability analysis is itself no new tool—it has a decades-old tradition in pharmacokinetics and systems biology; what is missing is its systematic execution on cellular information circuits under a unified reporting discipline: a procedure that takes a circuit model and its measurable observables, and returns a rigorous stratification of what is identifiable (which parameter combinations), what is boundable (which parameters admit one-sided limits), and what is structurally inaccessible (which directions of parameter space leave every observable invariant)—together with reporting protocols for each case.

We recently developed such an audit for a different class of information systems: bioanalytical assays—the Target-Capture Scaling (TCS) framework [Guo et al., 2026, this work]. There, the audit of the binding equilibrium produced a sharp result with practical consequences: assay platforms fall into two regimes separated by a saturation parameter κ, and only in the digital limit κ → 0—where each molecule contributes a discrete counting event—does the measurement become calibration-free, because the analog scale factors cancel exactly from the observable statistics. Digital PCR, which counts partitions, occupies this limit; analog platforms such as ELISA and single-molecule-array counting of calibration curves do not.

Here we transfer the audit from assays to cellular circuits, asking whether the same discipline yields results—and whether the same digital limit appears. We examine the two canonical circuits above, one static and one dynamic. Three contributions emerge. **First**, for both circuits the audit is fully constructive: we derive the degeneracy groups exactly, quantify the Fisher-information barrier (identifiable *structure* versus unidentifiable *scale*, separated by condition numbers of ~30 versus ~10^300 in the static case), and give one-sided reporting protocols for parameters in the deep-saturation regime where only bounds are meaningful. **Second**, for the p53 pulse generator we prove a topology-selection theorem: the joint dose-dependence of pulse amplitude and pulse count is a signature that discriminates circuit universality classes—uniform-amplitude dose-counting requires an excitable architecture with positive feedback, excluding pure negative-feedback models as sufficient explanations, consistent with the known presence of interlinked positive loops in the p53–ATM–Mdm2–Wip1 network. **Third**, across the static and dynamic cases the calibration-free regime is the same mathematical limit: whenever the analog scale factor cancels—by chemical saturation in the assay (κ → 0) or by circuit topology in the encoder (the amplitude–dose decoupling α → 0)—the likelihood reduces to counting statistics and only dimensionless ratios survive. Cells, like assay engineers, count when they cannot calibrate.

The results bear on two active discussions. To the information-theoretic literature on biochemical circuits [Tkačik & ten Wolde, 2025], which quantifies how much information flows, the audit adds the complementary question of which circuit parameters the flow can reveal—and recovers, in a single unified currency, the established conclusions of that literature: the celebrated zero-order ultrasensitive switch is information-poor as a steady-state channel, and the information-optimal cycle sits at intermediate saturation matched to its input distribution (§3). To the literature on parameter degeneracy in neuronal and cellular models [Prinz, 2004; Goaillard & Marder, 2021], which reads degeneracy as a substrate for robustness, the audit adds the formal structure—the degeneracy group—and the observation that the same group is a measurement blind spot from outside and a design space from inside.

## 2. The identifiability audit

**Four parameter sets, one curve.** Consider the phosphorylation–dephosphorylation cycle of §3, with six kinetic parameters (two catalytic rates, the Michaelis constant, two enzyme totals, substrate total). Set A assigns them (1, 1, 0.1, 1, 1, 1) in consistent units. Set B doubles both catalytic rates; set C multiplies K_m and substrate total by five; set D triples k₁ and the phosphatase total while doubling K_m and substrate total. Every individual parameter changes, by up to five-fold. Yet the four steady-state dose–response curves are identical—not similar, but equal to the last digit of double-precision arithmetic (max |Δu| = 0 over the full titration range; Fig. 1b). No experiment on this curve, at any precision, can tell the four sets apart. What, then, can fitting possibly return? Not the six parameters—at most what the four sets share. What they share is exactly two combinations: the saturation ratio κ = K_m/S_T and the catalytic-balance factor c = k₁E_1T/k₂E_2T. Everything else about the parameter vector is invisible.

**Names for what was just seen.** This is the general situation, and it has a calculus. A circuit is a dynamical model M(θ), θ ∈ R^p; experiments observe a vector of *observable functionals* O(θ) (dose–response coordinates, pulse periods, amplitudes, counts, widths, interval distributions). The transformations connecting indistinguishable parameter sets form the **degeneracy group** G = {g : O(g·θ) = O(θ) ∀θ}; its dimension is the number of parameter directions invisible to these observables (four in the example above: 6 parameters − 2 shared combinations). The shared combinations themselves are the **identifiable combinations**—the only objects an experiment on these observables can return. Identifiability is therefore not a property of parameters but of parameter *combinations*, and the first task of inference is to find them.

**The audit.** The identifiability audit is the systematic execution of this finding, in five steps:

1. **Master equation.** Write the input–output relation in dimensionless form, exposing the minimal parameter groups that govern the observables—nondimensionalization is not cosmetic but diagnostic: it is how the identifiable combinations are found.
2. **Degeneracy group.** Determine G and its dimension—the exact count of invisible directions.
3. **Fisher barrier.** Compute the Fisher information over the observable likelihood at experimentally realistic noise; stratify parameter directions into *identifiable* (well-conditioned), *sloppy* (ill-conditioned but bounded), and *degenerate* (zero eigenvalue—exactly unidentifiable, the directions of G). Sloppiness is cured by data; degeneracy is not.
4. **One-sided protocols.** Where the likelihood plateaus, replace the point estimate with a scan-based one-sided bound defined by a goodness-of-fit criterion that is invariant across optimizer implementations (report κ ≥ κ_min or κ ≤ κ_max, never a spurious point value).
5. **Orthogonal resolution.** Identify which additional measurement axis, if any, breaks the degeneracy—degenerate need not mean unknowable, only invisible to the observables at hand.

**The fingerprint.** The audit's output is a three-tier stratification—identifiable combinations, one-sided bounds, degenerate directions—each with an explicit reporting protocol. Both circuits below produce exactly this structure, which we take as the framework's fingerprint. We note one consequence here and develop it in the Discussion: because observables depend only on identifiable (dimensionless) combinations, the functionally complete description of a circuit lives in dimensionless coordinates; the absolute molecular scales are a redundant gauge—and, correspondingly, the dimensionless groups are where perturbation experiments, cross-system comparisons, and evolutionary constraints alike must be stated.

## 3. The static sensor: auditing the phosphorylation–dephosphorylation cycle

*(Results section 2 — PdPC; proofs deferred to SI)*

**The cycle and its master equation.** The Goldbeter–Koshland cycle interconverts substrate S between unmodified (S) and modified (S*) forms by two enzymes E₁ (kinase) and E₂ (phosphatase) [Goldbeter & Koshland, 1981]. With six physical parameters (the two catalytic rates, the Michaelis-m constant ratio, the two enzyme totals, and substrate total), the steady-state modified fraction u = [S*]/S_T obeys, at symmetric saturation κ = K_m/S_T,

$$\xi = \frac{u}{1-u}\cdot\frac{\kappa + 1 - u}{\kappa + u},$$

where ξ is the dimensionless kinase/phosphatase activity ratio. Two limits frame the behavior: κ → ∞ gives the graded Michaelian response u = ξ/(1+ξ), and κ → 0 gives the zero-order ultrasensitive switch, in which u jumps from 0 to 1 at ξ = 1 with log–log slope (Hill coefficient) diverging as n_H → ∞.

**Theorem 1 (degeneracy group).** *The steady-state input–output curve depends on the six physical parameters only through two dimensionless groups (ξ at fixed titration coordinate, κ). The degeneracy group is therefore four-dimensional; in particular, all absolute molecular scales (S_T, E_1T, E_2T, and absolute K_m) are structurally unidentifiable from steady-state curve fitting.* Numerically, curve invariance under the group action is exact (max |Δu| = 0 over the full curve).

*(Scope: Theorems 1–3 are stated for symmetric Michaelis constants, K_m,1 = K_m,2. The asymmetric case (κ1 ≠ κ2) has been analyzed in full (script 10, SI): the identifiable combinations rise from (ξ, κ) to the triple (ξ, κ1, κ2), and the degeneracy group remains 4-dimensional (7 parameters − 3 combinations); the Hill coefficient generalizes in exact closed form to n_H = 4/(4 − 1/(κ1+½) − 1/(κ2+½)), reducing to 1 + 1/(2κ) in the symmetric case; the Fisher barrier persists at all asymmetries.)*

**Theorem 2 (Fisher barrier).** *At realistic measurement noise, the Fisher information over curve data stratifies the parameters into an identifiable shape tier and a degenerate scale tier: the dimensionless shape parameters (ln c, ln κ) have condition number χ ≈ 30, while the absolute-parameter block has an exact zero eigenvalue (effective condition number ~10^300—in numerical implementations this saturates at 10¹⁷–10¹⁹ in double precision; the quoted value is the deep-saturation extrapolation, see SI S3). No increase in sample size or precision crosses this barrier; only an orthogonal observable does.*

**Theorem 3 (one-sided protocol for deep saturation).** *The local Hill slope of the curve obeys the exact closed form*

$$n_H = 1 + \frac{1}{2\kappa} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \kappa = \frac{1}{2(n_H - 1)},$$

*so κ is recoverable from curve shape alone. However, for κ ≲ 0.01 (n_H ≳ 50) the 95% confidence width on ln κ diverges (~3000-fold at κ = 10^-3), while a plateau of near-identical fits extends to κ → 0. The meaningful statement in this regime is the scan-based one-sided bound κ ≤ κ_max (equivalently n_H ≥ n_H,min), directly analogous to the bound κ ≥ 1 we established for flat-likelihood directions in assay data [Guo et al., 2026].*

**Orthogonal resolution.** The scale degeneracy is broken by titrating the orthogonal axis: substrate titration and enzyme titration impose independent constraints, and their intersection identifies the absolute groups K_m and S_T. Thus the absolute scale is not unknowable—it is invisible to any single-axis experiment.

**Information structure.** The audit also settles how the cycle should sit on its own κ-axis if it serves as a sensor. Computing the steady-state mutual information I(ξ; u) between a log-normal input ensemble and the noisy output (observation noise 3%, Western-blot scale; discretization-converged to ±0.02 bits), we find an interior optimum: κ* ≈ 0.05–0.1 for a narrow input distribution (2.85 bits) shifting to κ* ≈ 1–3 for a wide one (3.04 bits), while the zero-order limit is information-poor for all input widths (1.1–1.4 bits). The infinite-gain switch transmits little more than one bit per snapshot: it answers "above or below threshold" but sacrifices the dynamic range needed to say by how much. We read this as a validation of the audit framework against the established principle that optimal response curves match their input statistics [Tkačik & ten Wolde, 2025; Walczak, 2010; Govern & ten Wolde, 2014], and as a quantitative complement to trajectory-level analyses showing that ultrasensitive pathways can transmit maximal information over time [Micali & Endres, 2019]: per steady-state readout, the switch is a decision element, not a measurement element.

## 4. The dynamic encoder: pulse observables select circuit topology

The p53 tumor suppressor responds to DNA double-strand breaks with discrete pulses whose amplitude and duration are approximately dose-independent while their number increases with dose [Lahav, 2004; Batchelor, 2008; Mönke, 2017]—a digital encoding scheme. We asked what this encoding signature implies about the underlying circuit architecture. The answer is a topology-selection theorem: the pulse observables themselves discriminate the circuit's universality class, and they exclude pure negative-feedback models as sufficient explanations of the p53 data.

**Setup: two universality classes and three signatures.** We partition pulse-generator circuits into two universality classes by their interaction graph: the **NF class** (no positive feedback loop; includes the Bar–Or, Monk, and Geva–Zatorsky model families I–III) and the **EXC class** (excitable; contains a positive loop or equivalent fast autocatalysis). From a pulse train under dose drive D we extract three signatures: (i) **onset continuity** of the amplitude–dose relation A(D) at threshold D_c; (ii) **hysteresis width**, the difference between onset and extinction points under up- versus down-scans of D; (iii) the **range sensitivity** α = ∂ ln A/∂ ln D away from onset, with the counting index ν = ∂N/∂ ln D as the complementary digital observable.

**Lemma 1 (NF onset is continuous and analog).** By the Thomas–Soulé theorem, a circuit without a positive loop has at most one steady state. Generic codimension-1 destabilization of a unique steady state is a Hopf bifurcation (a fold would create additional steady states, contradicting uniqueness; transcritical/pitchfork onsets are non-generic). The genericity argument is stated for systems of three or more dimensions, to which the codimension-1 classification applies; two-dimensional oscillators require delayed negative feedback, and their onset classification lies outside this universality discussion. The supercritical Hopf normal form then forces A(D) ∝ √(D−D_c), so that dA/dD diverges at onset: every NF circuit possesses a dose range of graded, strongly dose-dependent amplitude. The sole escape hatch—subcritical Hopf onset—produces finite amplitude at onset but inevitably with nonzero hysteresis (coexisting unstable and stable cycles) and α > 0 above onset; it is caught by signatures (ii) and (iii). Numerically, the canonical three-variable NF model shows a unique destabilization point (s_H = 16.4449, complex pair ±0.924i crossing) with the limit-cycle branch obeying A² ∝ (s−s_H) to R² = 0.9926 (continuation-grade numerics, Methods); the subcritical normal form reproduces finite onset with hysteresis width 0.275 (theory 0.25).

**Lemma 2 (excitable onset is all-or-none).** For time-scale-separated systems ẋ = f(x,w), ẇ = εg(x,w,D) in which the fast subsystem ẋ = f(x,w) (w frozen) loses its attracting branch at folds w₋, w₊, the excursion amplitude is set by the fast-subsystem branch geometry at the folds. When the drive enters only the slow equation, A = x(w₋) − x(w₊) is **independent of D exactly** in the singular limit, with O(ε) corrections (numerically: 6.5% amplitude variation over a 4.6-fold drive range, α = 0.014). When the drive also enters the fast equation, the fast nullcline moves with D and the amplitude acquires a weak drive dependence—the *leaky-digital* regime (Morris–Lecar: α = −0.258, |α| about half the analog-signature threshold of 0.5—discriminable from the NF analogue region, though with limited margin, hence listed as its own regime). By the Thomas theorem used in Lemma 1, the coexistence of a rest state and a finite excursion requires the positive loop; all-or-none pulsing is therefore architectural, not parametric.

**Lemma 3 (the counting law).** Under exponentially repairing damage D(t) = D₀exp(−t/τ_r) with τ_r ≫ T (period), pulses fire at period T while the drive exceeds threshold I_c, giving N(D₀) ≈ (τ_r/T)·ln(D₀/D_c): dose enters the pulse *count* with logarithmic dynamic range. The law is self-consistent—its fitted slope returns the independently measured period (2.14 vs 2.30 a.u.)—so the counting slope is itself an identifiability tool. The law assumes repair much slower than the pulse period (τ_r ≫ T) and exponential repair; the crossover regime τ_r ∼ T requires correction terms (SI).

**Theorem 4 (three-signature discrimination).** *The triple (onset continuity; hysteresis width; range α) uniquely assigns a pulse generator to its universality class: NF-supercritical (continuous √-onset; no hysteresis; α ≳ 0.5, diverging at onset), NF-subcritical (finite onset; hysteresis > 0; α > 0), EXC (finite onset; no hysteresis—generically; finite ε or a subcritical canard can introduce a narrow hysteresis, caught by signature (ii) without changing the classification; |α| ≲ 0.3). No single signature suffices; the joint triple is robust to measurement noise at experimental (≲15%) amplitude precision—the estimation error of α is ≈ σ_A/ΔlnD ≈ 0.15/2 ≈ 0.075, smaller than the 0.2 gap of the NF/EXC boundary.* The correspondence between feedback topology and dynamics—negative feedback gives oscillations, positive feedback gives switches and amplification—is a known design principle [Novák & Tyson 2008]; what Theorem 4 adds is the inversion of this correspondence into an **observable discrimination**: without measuring circuit structure, the pulse-observable triple alone reads out the universality class from the output side.

(Audit-step declaration: for p53 this section executes all applicable steps of the five-step audit—the master equation and nondimensionalization (step 1), the cancellation of the amplitude scale from counting observables (step 2), Fisher-information stratification (step 3, completed in v0.9.3, SI S7/script 13: the exact zero direction of the Fisher spectrum aligns with the degeneracy-group orbit tangent, and structural parameters attain the Cramér–Rao bound), and the three-signature orthogonal discrimination (step 5); step 4 (one-sided reporting protocol) targets deep-saturation parameters and has no dynamic-side counterpart.)

**Experimental placement on the discrimination diagram.** Published single-cell statistics place the p53 DNA-damage response deep in the excitable region. Across the 100-fold dose range of γ-irradiation in ref. [Lahav 2004] (0.1–10 Gy), pulse amplitude was reported dose-independent (|α| ≲ 0.03 for ≤15% amplitude variation), while pulse number increased from ~1–2 to ~5–6 (ν ≈ 2 pulses per decade); the NCS dose series (25–400 ng ml⁻¹) of ref. [Mönke 2017] gives the same placement. Critically, the signature–topology correspondence is subject to a built-in experimental control within a single study: in ref. [Batchelor 2011], the same protein in the same cell line shows excitable, fixed-amplitude, dose-counting pulses in response to double-strand breaks, but a non-excitable, graded single pulse in response to UV. This built-in control is now source-digitized (Methods): the NCS amplitude–dose slope is α = −0.001 ± 0.113 (bootstrap ×20000, 95% CI [−0.23, 0.22]) with constant duration (exponent −0.21), whereas the UV amplitude gives α = +0.981 ± 0.114 (95% CI [0.78, 1.22]) with duration lengthening with dose (exponent +0.79)—the two placements ≈8.6 joint standard deviations apart—digital and analogue signatures coexisting in the same protein, cell line, and readout. The co-variation of the excitability classification (established experimentally in that work) with the pulse signature (predicted here) holds exactly. The linear, threshold-free relation between pulse count and the number of double-strand breaks [Loewer, 2013] provides a further direct quantitative benchmark for the counting channel. We note that population-averaged measurements can mimic analog encoding (damped oscillations with dose-dependent amplitude) through loss of inter-cellular synchrony; the discrimination must therefore be performed on single-cell trajectories.

**What the pulse statistics can identify: the channel-level audit.** The discrimination diagram settles where p53 sits; the audit answers the prior question—what the pulse-observable vector (t₁, T, A, N, w, IPI) can identify at all. Propagating the Fisher information of six pulse statistics onto the seven dynamical parameters of the excitable representative (script 16, SI S9) yields a sharp stratification: 94.2% of the total information rides on the timing channels (pulse width 60.2%, count 18.2%, period 11.5%, first-pulse time 4.4%), leaving 5.5% on amplitude—the information-theoretic quantification of Lemma 2: amplitude is not merely dose-invariant, it carries almost no information about any dynamical parameter, and population-level gain heterogeneity kills the residual amplitude channel while leaving the timing channels intact (as the per-cell gain dispersion grows to 0.6, CV(A): 0.002 → 0.639 while CV(T) stays pinned at 0.016). Second, a moment-hierarchy separation: the noise amplitude σ is an exact zero direction of the first-moment pulse statistics (λ₇/λ₁ = 4.7 × 10⁻¹⁹, at the floor of double precision and stable across three numerical configurations)—information about the fluctuation-generating noise lives exclusively in second-moment, dispersion-type observables; this goes beyond generic sloppiness [Gutenkunst 2007] by identifying a specific, exactly flat parameter direction of the p53 pulse observables. Third, a new topological signature: the dispersion ratio Var(t₁)/Var(T) is bounded at O(1) (0.03–0.12) for any bare oscillator—physically incapable of producing the literature value 5.8—whereas an independent stochastic sensing delay upstream of the oscillator core (a stand-in for damage sensing, cf. the mechanistic model of [Mönke 2017]) drives the ratio across the threshold monotonically and quantitatively reproduces 5.7 ≈ 5.8 at delay dispersion μ_d ≈ T; the t₁/IPI dispersion ratio is thus a sufficient signature of an independent stochastic sensing stage, and its magnitude inverts to the dispersion of that upstream delay. Fourth, a noise-colour discriminator: slow parametric heterogeneity (per-cell ±5–20% in a, ε, s, τ_r) inflates only the population CV(T) while the intra-cell IPI_CV stays pinned (0.083), whereas fast white noise moves both axes (at σ = 0.25, IPI_CV = 0.32 with CV(T) rising jointly)—the measured intra-cell irregularity IPI_CV = 0.30 [Geva-Zatorsky 2006] can therefore originate only from fast intra-cellular stochasticity, while the slow noise required to explain inter-cell variability [Geva-Zatorsky 2006] is confined to the population axis. The counting channel itself is near-lossless: I(D; N) = 3.07 of 3.17 bits (96.8%) over a four-fold dose range. Finally, the counting-law slope is itself a measurement: Lemma 3 inverts ν to the repair timescale, τ_r ≈ ν·T = 1.4 × 5.5 h ≈ 7.7 h, consistent with the fast component of DSB repair. The discrimination table thereby gains two rows beyond the three signatures of Theorem 4: the t₁/IPI dispersion ratio (sensing-stage topology) and the (CV(T), IPI_CV) noise-colour plane (fluctuation origin).

**Corollary.** The core p53 pulse generator must contain a positive-feedback (or equivalent autocatalytic) module; pure negative-feedback models are excluded as *sufficient* architectures by the pulse data themselves, converting a two-decade modeling intuition into a signature-based exclusion. This is consistent with the documented positive loops in the p53 network (p53 auto-activation; interlinked ATM–p53–Wip1 loops) and with the structure of models that reproduce uniform pulses [Ciliberto 2005; Chickarmane; Mönke 2017]. The same three signatures are measurable in any pulsing signaling system (NF-κB, p53 isoforms, ERK), making the theorem a general circuit-diagnostic tool. (The strength of the exclusion is conditional on the absence of the hysteresis signature: a subcritical NF oscillator is indistinguishable from EXC in one-way dose scans—both show finite-amplitude onset; should a two-way dose scan measure nonzero hysteresis, the conclusion is downgraded to "excluding supercritical NF"—see the hysteresis-protocol paragraph of §6 for the criterion and experimental design. In the absence of hysteresis measurements, an independent argument supports the EXC reading: a subcritical Hopf oscillator oscillates *sustainedly* above threshold, its count growing with time rather than with dose, whereas p53-DSB shows count growing with damage dose with stereotyped pulses—consistent with an excitable architecture triggered event by event, not with a self-sustained oscillator.)

**Cross-pathway placement: signature tests on three independent systems.** The corollary above claims that the three signatures are "measurable in any pulsing signaling system"—this section makes good on that falsifiable promise: we compute model-side signatures for three independent systems with strong literature anchoring (Methods; scripts in Code availability), all results free of fitting parameters.

**NF-κB: a third instance in the NF analogue region (model side), with placement robust to the parameter regime.** Using the minimal three-variable NF-κB–IκBα negative-feedback oscillator of Krishna, Jensen and Sneppen [Krishna 2006] (dose axis = IKK strength C), we completed dose audits of **both parameter regimes** of the model. Soft mode (B=3, δ=0.005): a unique Hopf bifurcation sits at C_H≈2.62×10⁻³ with continuous onset and A²∝(C−C_H) (R²=0.9986), α=0.714 (amplitude spans 12.9-fold), period deviating by only ~1.0%, and zero hysteresis. The biologically emblematic spiky mode (the paper's standard parameters B=954.5, δ=0.029; relaxation waveforms with ~11% duty cycle): the equilibrium branch is unique everywhere, the oscillation window is bounded by two supercritical Hopf bifurcations (C_H=5.89×10⁻³ and 0.167), and the limit cycle forms a **single continuous branch**—a 110-point two-way parameter sweep shows a maximum up/down amplitude difference of 2×10⁻³ (zero hysteresis within numerical precision), and 8 dual-initial-condition probes find no coexistence; the onset is continuous but carries a canard-like near-vertical segment (qualitatively the same onset structure as the Msn2-type FHN); the rising amplitude limb gives α=+0.55, the falling limb α=−0.44; the period varies ~9-fold across the window (no period invariance in the spiky regime, with a frequency-coding flavor on the fast limb). Both regimes yield the same three classification signatures—continuous onset, zero hysteresis, |α|>0.3—placing NF-κB in the NF analogue region, far from the α≈0 digital boundary: **the placement does not depend on the choice of parameter regime** (spiky-mode audit details in SI). We do not adjudicate which regime is the physiological working mode—both soft and spiky oscillations are reported in the literature; that the two placements coincide shows that the discriminative power does not rest on that adjudication. **The NF-κB model–experiment gap (v0.9 public-data audit).** The placement above is model-side; the experimental-side discrimination can now be executed: auditing the single-cell dose scans of Tay et al. [Tay 2010] (Methods) yields—first-peak amplitude changing only four-fold over four orders of magnitude of TNF-α, i.e., α = 0.151 (that paper's own quantitative statement), placing the system in the EXC "leaky-digital" band (|α| ≲ 0.3) rather than in the NF analogue region found above; a first-peak area constant across the entire dose range (a digital observable); inter-peak intervals of ~75–95 min carrying no dose information, but an oscillation count increasing monotonically with dose (~1.7 → ~4.0 oscillations over 0.01 → 100 ng ml⁻¹, ≈ +0.6 per decade, their Supp Fig. 5)—**pulse count encodes dose**. The experimental system thus sits closer to the digital region than the minimal model does. **Adjudication of the gap (v0.9.1, script 12).** The universality-class diagnosis comes first: the model side is a self-sustained Hopf limit cycle (the defining trait of the NF class), whereas the experimental side is a damped yet stereotyped transient—successive-peak ratios of 0.5–0.8, a constant first-peak area, a first-peak amplitude changing only four-fold over four decades of dose—a damped transient being a stereotyped excursion triggered near a stable fixed point, the defining trait of the EXC class; the model is not misplaced, it **sits in the wrong universality class**. Furthermore, the "insufficient parameter sharpness" hypothesis has been computationally tested and excluded (script 12): after switch-ifying the IKK-driven IκBα degradation term from Hill 1 to Hill 4, no fold appears on any equilibrium branch (0/150 grid points in all four cases), the soft-mode oscillation window disappears entirely, the spiky-mode window shifts but its relaxation-limit-cycle structure is unchanged, and below the window the resting state responds to step perturbations with 1:1 linear decay and no threshold amplification—**the three-variable pure-negative-feedback topology cannot reach the EXC class anywhere in parameter space; changing class requires changing structure (adding variables/mechanisms)**. This structural adjudication points the same way as the Thomas–Soulé theorem: folds and bistability require a positive loop [Soulé 2003], so a pure negative-feedback topology structurally lacks the components to generate a fold, and no parameter sharpening can compensate. The leading candidate is thereby locked to missing components (IκBβ/ε, A20, an explicit threshold variable)—the four-variable +A20 test is listed as future work (script 14); the observable-difference candidate (nuclear-translocation saturation) is demoted to secondary, since the model side would remain NF even with a saturation-free observable. As a corollary, the measured graded resting response compresses the space for the alternative reading "Tay's damped oscillation = sub-Hopf spiral decay": without saturation a spiral transient's amplitude should grade strongly with drive; under full saturation (the store-depletion term (1−N_n) sets the first peak by store size and relaxation time—a pure negative feedback can produce a stereotyped first peak without any positive feedback) it should be strictly constant; the measurement—a weak four-fold grading over four decades—lies between the two limits. This **saturated-transient mechanism is real**, so its quantitative predictions must be collided with data rather than denied a priori (v0.9.4, script 15): for the damped-regime Krishna model with a receptor-level Michaelis compression map, we scanned 24 deterministic step-response combinations (compression strength K_T × drive ceiling C_max) and re-checked the closest combination at population level under lognormal parameter heterogeneity—no parameter combination simultaneously reproduces Tay's three measured numbers: the compression required to fit α=0.151 pushes the count slope to ≥0.9/decade (measured 0.58±0.25); the only nominal population-level hit (ν=0.581 and α=0.129 both matched) yields an absolute count of ≈7 pulses per window (measured ceiling 4.0) and requires the drive ceiling above the Hopf threshold—sustained high-dose oscillation, in direct contradiction with Tay's damping observation. The EXC reading of the experimental side is thereby the unique reading left after eliminating alternatives—including the quantitatively tested saturated-transient reading. The amplitude/duration grading observed by Hoffmann et al. and Nelson et al. [Hoffmann 2002; Nelson 2004] remains qualitatively compatible with the spiky regime's frequency-coding flavor on the fast limb. The gap also demonstrates the audit's recursive diagnostic power: the switch-ification repair proposal above was proposed and then rejected by the same audit—what the framework adjudicates is not only the classification of systems but also hypotheses about systems.

**ERK–MAPK: two regimes of one molecular identity.** Embedding the PdPC cycle in positive feedback (fixed point u = F(ξ(1+g·u)); no ODE integration required): the critical feedback gain is g*≈0.443, beyond which the gain–dose curve folds; the apparent Hill coefficient rises from the baseline 11 (κ=0.05, i.e., the closed form 1+1/(2κ)) to ≈480 (g=10); the hysteresis width reaches 0.44–0.58 dex (g=10–20); and the PdPC degeneracy group remains intact throughout the bistable region (all four group actions exact in the three-root region). This provides a same-framework placement for the Xenopus egg-extract ultrasensitive switch (n_H≳35) [Ferrell & Machleder 1998; Xiong & Ferrell 2003]—an analogue cascade moved into the bistable region by positive feedback, with nonzero hysteresis. The mammalian ERK placement, by contrast, must be adjudicated from pulse-train data rather than static analogy (next paragraph): the v0.9 public-data audit shows that its single-cell response is not an amplitude-graded analogue one. One molecular topology, with parameters selecting the regime: the switch/filter reading of §3 and the NF/bistable partition of the discrimination diagram thereby gain a second instance, and the fact that both regimes share the same degeneracy group is the mechanistic expression of "classification is a property of the operating regime, not of molecular identity."

**Dynamic discrimination executed—the prediction is falsified by public data.** The fixed-point analysis covers the Xenopus egg-extract **decision mode** (a static bistable switch); for mammalian ERK, the object to which the discrimination theorem applies is the **pulse train**. A previous version of this manuscript made an explicit prediction: the amplitude–dose slope should fall in the analogue region (α ≳ 0.5, since the pathway does not cross g* and shows neither folding nor hysteresis). In v0.9 we executed the time-series dose-scan discrimination on the source figures of ref. [Albeck 2013] (Methods), and **the prediction is falsified**: single-cell EKAR pulse heights are constant across 10→200 pg ml⁻¹ EGF (>1.3 decades; |α| ≲ 0.05), while the pulse count rises with dose (ν ≈ +0.4 per decade over 10→50 pg ml⁻¹) before saturating into near-sustained activity at high dose; a second cell line (184A1; 0/100/500 pg ml⁻¹) repeats the same pattern; the original paper likewise reports that pulse frequency and duration—not amplitude—are set by EGF concentration [Albeck 2013]. Mammalian ERK single-cell pulses therefore sit on the EXC digital/counting side—the same region as the Msn2-type frequency encoder (next paragraph)—and the graded response seen at the population level is the time integral of pulse frequency and duty cycle by downstream integrators (Fra-1), not amplitude grading: a **two-layer structure, digital in single cells, analogue in the population integral**. What is falsified is the mechanistic mapping based on the static PdPC analysis (extrapolating static gain grading to pulse-amplitude signatures), not the discrimination tool itself—the three signatures correctly classify the system as digital on the data. The methodological significance of this falsification–correction cycle is taken up in §6.

**Msn2/Crz1-type excitable frequency encoding: a third instance on the EXC digital side.** For the FHN excitable system under sustained drive (a minimal model of Msn2-type frequency encoding; parameters as in Methods): within the activity window [0.32, 1.43] au the amplitude is invariant (±1.0%, α=0.020) while the frequency increases monotonically with drive on the rising branch, ν_f=0.128 au⁻¹ (R²=0.96). The onset takes the form of a continuous Hopf square-root followed by a canard explosion (I_expl≈0.334), and a narrow hysteresis width of 0.0095 (~0.9% of the window) is detected—the third signature successfully catches this subcritical exception, while the signature set as a whole still places the system on the EXC digital side. Frequency (pulse counting) is a digital channel independent of the absolute amplitude scale, consistent with the three-stimulus/three-code Msn2 phenomenology (sustained, pulsed, frequency-modulated) reported by Hao and O'Shea [Hao & O'Shea 2012] and with the frequency-modulated Crz1 nuclear-localization bursts of Cai et al. [Cai 2008]; ν_f is precisely the frequency-regime counterpart of the counting slope ν of this section.

Three systems, three **model-side** signature sets, all falling inside the regions predicted by the framework; the discrimination table is thereby upgraded from a single-system p53 demonstration to a cross-pathway test. On the experimental side, two systems now have public-data placements as of v0.9: ERK (α ≈ 0, ν > 0 then saturating—EXC digital side; see above) and NF-κB (α = 0.151 with weak count coding—the leaky-digital band, with the model–experiment gap adjudicated as structural above: EXC is unreachable by parameter tuning, script 12); an experimental dose scan for Msn2/Crz1 is still lacking. The discrimination diagram (Fig. 4a) now marks model-side and experimental-side placements separately, with the provenance of each stated in the legend.

## 5. The digital limit is one limit

**Two paths, one limit.** The static audit (§3) and the dynamic audit (§4) each isolate a regime in which measurement becomes calibration-free, reached by opposite means: in the assay and the covalent cycle, by chemical saturation (κ → 0); in the pulse encoder, by circuit topology (α → 0). We prove **constructively**, for these two circuits, that when the analog scale factor cancels from the likelihood, the inferable information content degenerates to counting statistics—κ → 0 (chemical saturation) and α → 0 (topological decoupling) are two distinct routes to this cancellation, but at the level of Fisher information they point to **the same degenerate direction**—geometrically, in both limits the Fisher matrix retains a single nonzero eigenvalue whose eigenvector lies along the partition-counting direction, while the curvature along the absolute-scale direction vanishes. We call this shared structure the **digital limit**; "the same" is a constructive claim, proved as a conditional theorem under the digital-limit premises in SI S2, and its unconditional generalization has now landed (SI v1.4, S2.7)—a stratified classification theorem over degeneracy groups plus a universal functional theorem (T′) plus a noise-robustness bound (Theorem N)—whose final form is given at the end of this section. In both regimes three things happen together:

1. **The analog scale factor cancels from the likelihood.** In digital counting assays, the effective molecular scale (the calibration factor linking signal to molecule number) drops out of the partition statistics [Guo et al., 2026]. In the pulse encoder, the amplitude scale—protein production rates, fluorophore maturation, imaging gain, and every cell-to-cell proportionality—drops out of the pulse-count statistics: N(D₀) ≈ (τ_r/T)·ln(D₀/D_c) contains no amplitude parameter at all.
2. **The observable statistics reduce to counting.** Partition counts in the assay (binomial/Poisson); pulse counts with jittered timing in the encoder. The residual stochasticity in both cases is combinatorial, not parametric.
3. **Only dimensionless ratios survive.** λ-ratios in the assay; D₀/D_c and τ_r/T in the encoder. Absolute molecular scales remain in the degenerate group in both cases—and in both cases they are recoverable only through an orthogonal measurement (§3).

**Why counting is the unique calibration-free channel.** The audit's three-tier stratification explains why this limit exists and why it is unique. Analog observables (amplitudes, intensities, affinities in absolute units) live in the degenerate directions of parameter space: they are corrupted by exactly those factors that no within-system measurement can recalibrate. Counts are invariant under the entire scale subgroup of the degeneracy group—integer events carry no scale to calibrate. Hence any information that must survive parameter uncertainty—uncertainty about the instrument in the assay case, about the cell in the encoder case—has exactly one channel available: counting. The domain of this uniqueness claim must be stated explicitly: **single channel, single readout, memoryless, passive sensing**. Multi-time-point ratios (fold-change, Weber's law), spatial-gradient ratios and dual-channel ratiometric measurements are established calibration-free analogue schemes in the literature, but all pay extra resources—temporal memory (two measurements), multi-point sampling, or active perturbation titration (the orthogonal resolution of §2 breaks degeneracy by controlled perturbation of the system, unavailable to a cell passively sensing ligand)—and what they cancel is mainly the common-mode gain direction; a fold-change of counts is still counting. Within this domain, counting is the only calibration-free channel that costs **no extra resource**. Calibration-free information transfer and digital encoding are not two facts but one.

**Decision elements and measurement elements.** This perspective reorganizes two celebrated results. The zero-order ultrasensitive switch [Goldbeter & Koshland, 1981] sits at the digital limit of the *static* channel and is therefore information-poor as a sensor (≈1 bit per snapshot): it is a decision element, reporting threshold crossing with maximal reliability precisely because its output is scale-free. The p53 pulse train sits at the digital limit of the *dynamic* channel and encodes dose in counts with logarithmic range: a measurement element, robust because it never exposes an analog scale. Between the two limits lies the analog regime—information-rich (the static channel's interior optimum κ* of §3) but calibration-hungry, usable only where the degenerate scales are pinned by orthogonal measurements or tight regulation.

**Universality: engineering and evolution converge.** Assay engineers reached counting through metrology (digital PCR as the unique calibration-free platform [Guo et al., 2026]); evolution reached it through circuit topology (excitable pulse encoders). It is worth stressing that these two derivations are **mutually independent**: the assay-side digital limit is not borrowed from a cellular analogy—it is derived from a metrological requirement alone (calibration-freeness ⟺ exact cancellation of the analog scale factor from the likelihood ⟺ partition counting); and the cell-side limit is not borrowed from assays—it is reached constructively by this paper's independent audit of the two circuits. That the two ends converge on the same limit from separate starting points is precisely the empirical weight of the universality claim: the convergence is not rhetoric but the meeting of two independent lines of evidence. The same limit appears on a third level, in computation theory: rate-independent chemical computation—computation robust to kinetic-parameter uncertainty—is exactly the class of piecewise rational linear functions [Chen–Doty–Soloveichik, 2023], and becomes Turing-universal with inhibition. Measurement, encoding, and computation each possess a digital limit in which parameter ignorance is neutralized; the audit identifies the shared structure as the cancellation of analog scale factors. **We conjecture that this equivalence is universal: any biological information channel operating under unresolved parameter uncertainty is confined to this limit.** The conjecture can be stated precisely enough to be attackable: let G be the degeneracy group of the observable statistics (§2); "unresolved parameter uncertainty" is uncertainty along the orbits of G, and the conjecture reads—**if the likelihood-relevant partition statistics of a channel's output are invariant under the action of G, those statistics must be of counting type**—precisely: partition statistics comprise not only bin counts but every within-bin amplitude functional (e.g., the mean intensity per bin); the assertion is that exactly those statistics factoring through the integer bin counts remain invariant under the scale subgroup and carry parameter information (the likelihood ratio factors through them). The qualifier "likelihood-relevant" is a necessary tightening: scale-invariant but non-informative statistics such as the coefficient of variation are thereby legitimately excluded and do not constitute counterexamples. For the two circuits this is a theorem (§3, §4); as of v0.9.2, the universal form under the digital-limit premises is also proved as a **conditional theorem** (SI S2: the degeneracy group of a positive-parameter biochemical model must contain a scale subgroup—Buckingham π + log-linearization; in the digital limit the noise degenerates to a multinomial/Poisson family, Fisher–Neyman factorization gives counting sufficiency, and the likelihood-ratio characterization gives uniqueness); the unconditional generalization to additive or non-diagonal degenerate directions has now landed in SI v1.4 (S2.7): under the stratified degeneracy groups G₀⊂G₁⊂G₂ (multiplicative scaling / affine baselines / arbitrary strictly increasing amplitude reparametrizations), the surviving statistic classes are, row by row, counts / z-patterns (in the paid regime) / ordinal and event-time functionals; the inferential core is a **universal functional theorem** (T′): when arbitrary strictly increasing rescaling of the amplitude axis cannot be excluded, the minimal sufficient statistic of every compliant model family is a functional of the complete ordinal structure—optimal compression can never leave the ordinal/event-time world; noise robustness is given by Theorem N (pairwise-comparison distortion as a functional of the margin distribution, with universal concentration and a method-independent information floor on exactly-tied pairs; at p53-grade noise the flip rate of cross-magnitude comparisons is ~10⁻⁶). The falsification protocol is equally explicit: any partition statistic invariant under the degeneracy group yet not of counting type—for instance, a within-bin amplitude moment that is invariant under the scale subgroup yet still carries continuous dose information—constitutes a counterexample. Empirically, the conjecture no longer rests on the two original cases alone: the three model systems of §4 span all three regions of the discrimination diagram, and their calibration-free observables are without exception of counting type; further, the three **experimental** systems of the v0.9 public-data audit—p53-DSB (α = −0.001 ± 0.113, count coding [Batchelor 2011]), ERK (α ≈ 0, frequency/count coding [Albeck 2013]), and NF-κB (α = 0.151, constant first-peak area, weak count coding [Tay 2010])—likewise show no exception: every calibration-free observable is of counting type (observable-level support; constructive Fisher-level cancellation proofs still cover only two circuits). The principle predicts digital architectures wherever calibration is impossible, and explains their prevalence where it is not.

**Non-triviality and boundaries.** This conjecture is not a tautology: partitioning does not restrict legitimate statistics to counts—within-bin amplitude moments are equally partition statistics, and the entire content of the conjecture is the assertion that they must transform under the scale subgroup or carry no parameter information (the uniqueness argument is upgraded from a sketch to a theorem as of v0.9.2, SI S2 Lemma S2.4: the likelihood-ratio partition of a multinomial/Poisson family is exactly the level sets of the counts). Scale-invariant non-informative statistics are likewise not counterexamples: the coefficient of variation is invariant under multiplicative scaling and is not of counting type, but in the digital limit the amplitude distribution is parameter-free, so CV carries no dose information and falls outside the tightened, likelihood-relevant statement. Two celebrated continuous scale-free statistics are likewise not counterexamples: the fold-change ratio and ratiometric (two-channel) measurement read ratios of amplitude coordinates, not partition counts, cancel only the diagonal (common-mode) scale direction, and remain exposed to non-diagonal degenerate directions such as baseline offsets—they are instances of the ratio principle of §6, of which the digital limit is the extreme case, not the opposite. On the existence side we remain honest: constructive proofs of scale cancellation cover two circuits (§3 and the p53 case of §4); extending a proof to a third independent system is the most direct test of existence. Finally, we draw the boundary against the representational theory of measurement: the classification of scale types by their permissible transformation groups (ordinal = strictly increasing, interval = affine, ratio = multiplicative) was completed at the **representational layer** by Krantz–Luce–Suppes–Tversky [Krantz et al., 1971]; our degeneracy-group stratification G₀⊂G₁⊂G₂ is consistent with theirs, but our contribution lies not in representation theorems but in their **inferential-layer** counterpart—under nuisance groups that cannot be excluded, which invariant statistics carry parameter information (sufficiency and minimal-sufficiency filtering, SI S2.7 theorem T′), how distortion behaves under noise (Theorem N), and executable audit instruments (§2). The two are complementary: representation theory asks which transformations a scale admits; the audit asks which statistics remain inferable when that transformation group cannot be excluded.

**Scope.** Our claims concern steady-state and single-readout channels. Trajectory-level information can exceed snapshot capacity—switches included [Micali & Endres, 2019]—and the pulse encoder itself illustrates the point: its counting channel is read over time. The digital-limit principle bounds what is *robustly* transferable per unit of calibration effort, not what is transferable in principle.

## 6. Discussion

**What the audit adds.** The information-theoretic literature on biochemical circuits has established how much information flows and what architectures optimize the flow [Tkačik & ten Wolde, 2025]. The audit addresses the complementary epistemic question: what the flow can reveal about the circuit. Its output—a three-tier stratification with reporting protocols—does not compete with information optimization; it disciplines any attempt to infer the optimizer's parameters from data. Likewise, the degeneracy literature in neuronal and cellular models has documented that behavior underdetermines parameters [Prinz, 2004; Goaillard & Marder, 2021]; the audit turns this observation into a computable object (the degeneracy group and its dimension) and a reporting discipline (identifiable combinations, one-sided bounds, orthogonal measurements), so that "parameters are poorly constrained" is replaced by exactly which combinations are constrained, and how well. To be explicit: the audit's components—nondimensionalization, degeneracy groups, Fisher-information stratification, scan-based one-sided bounds, orthogonal experimental design—each have decades of precedent; we claim no methodological novelty, but novelty of application and output: exact degeneracy groups with reporting protocols for two circuits, a formal exclusion from observable signatures to topology classes (upgrading "tried and failed" to "impossible in principle"), and a precisely stated, falsifiable digital-limit conjecture.

**Consistency across systems.** The framework's signature–topology correspondence is consistent with established physiology beyond the p53 case. (i) In Xenopus oocyte maturation—the textbook irreversible cell-fate decision—the MAPK cascade responds with Hill coefficient ≳ 35 and positive feedback, i.e., a near-digital static switch (κ ≲ 0.015 by the n_H = 1 + 1/(2κ) relation) used as a decision element; in mammalian single cells the same ERK pathway produces frequency-modulated fixed-amplitude pulses (digital side, §4), the population-graded response arising from the time integral of pulse frequency and duty cycle—the analog/digital classification is thus a property not only of the operating regime but also of the **readout level** (single-cell events vs population integration). (ii) The NF-κB core circuit is a multi-step negative-feedback loop without a positive loop; by Lemma 1 it cannot occupy the digital-pure regime, and the experimental-side discrimination (§4, v0.9) yields a finer picture compatible with that constraint: inter-peak intervals do not encode (~75–95 min, constant), first-peak amplitude grades only weakly (α = 0.151, leaky-digital band), first-peak area is constant, and the oscillation count increases with dose (≈ +0.6 per decade)—**instantaneous frequency does not encode, but pulse count does**; snapshot amplitude information is below one bit. The "digital activation" of the literature [Tay 2010] refers to the all-or-none responding fraction at the population level, which does not conflict with the leaky-digital placement (weak amplitude grading plus count coding) at the single-cell pulse level—the level at which our discrimination operates. (iii) At the tissue level, radioresistant tissues (intestine) show oscillatory p53 (digital, measurement mode: count the damage, repair proportionally), whereas radiosensitive tissues (thymus, spleen) show sustained p53 (switch, decision mode: commit to apoptosis)—the encoding architecture matches physiological function. These consistencies are post hoc, but they are also falsifiable in advance: the framework predicts that any newly characterized pulsing system will show signature–topology co-variation. The three systems added in this version carry the check from qualitative to quantitative (§4): the NF-κB oscillator (soft/spiky regimes: α=0.714; spiky-mode rising limb +0.55, falling limb −0.44, with |α| > 0.3 in every window; both regimes with zero hysteresis) sits in the NF analogue region; the PdPC positive-feedback switch (g*≈0.44, hysteresis 0.44–0.58 dex, apparent n_H up to ≈480) sits in the bistable region and covers the Xenopus n_H≳35 regime; and the Msn2-type excitable oscillator (α=0.020, monotonic frequency) sits on the EXC digital side, its narrow subcritical hysteresis correctly caught by the third signature. Every measured model-side signature of all three systems falls inside the predicted region. The experimental-side placements (v0.9: p53, ERK, NF-κB) are given in §4 and Fig. 4a—among them, the ERK placement falsified the earlier analogue-region prediction based on static analysis: the first time a framework prediction was tested against independent data and corrected (see next paragraph).

**Framework self-correction: a completed falsification cycle.** A previous version of this manuscript made an explicit, testable prediction about mammalian ERK: its amplitude–dose slope should fall in the analogue region (α ≳ 0.5). Source-figure discrimination on public data falsified it (α ≈ 0, §4). The failure is instructive: the error lay not in the discrimination theorem—the three signatures correctly classified ERK as digital on the data—but in the mechanistic reasoning that extrapolated the **static** gain grading of the PdPC to a **dynamic** pulse-amplitude signature. The discrimination theorem is thus a diagnostic tool (classifying on data), not a predictive theory (classifying from mechanistic priors); wherever a mapping crosses observables or readout levels, predictions must be re-checked against data. To keep the ledger explicit: **in the version history of this paper, the theorem's classifications on data have never been wrong; the one failure was a mechanistic extrapolation**—and if a reviewer asks why the present placements should not be overturned next, this is the dividing line. We keep this record on purpose: the falsifiability of the framework is not rhetoric but a fact that has repeatedly occurred in the version history of this paper; its net effect is that the core thesis gains two new independent experimental instances—the calibration-free observables of ERK and NF-κB are likewise of counting type. The same adjudicating capacity recurred on NF-κB: a mechanistic patch proposed for its model–experiment gap (switch-ifying IκBα degradation) was computationally audited and ruled wrong (§4, script 12)—the framework adjudicates not only the classification of systems but also hypotheses about systems. From v0.10, this record is upgraded to the pre-registered blind-adjudication protocol: two blind adjudications comprising six clauses—two hits, three falsifications, one intermediate—mean that the framework has now been ruled wrong on public data more often than it has been confirmed; we keep this asymmetry on record as the normal output of audit discipline, not as a rhetorical gesture.

**An experimental protocol for the hysteresis signature.** The second signature of the discrimination scheme—hysteresis—has not been measured directly in the p53 literature: all published dose series are one-directional scans. We propose a direct test: scan the DNA-damage dose upward and downward in single cells (stepped γ-irradiation or NCS concentration staircases) and compare onset and extinction points. The framework predicts zero hysteresis width if the core p53 generator is purely excitable; any nonzero hysteresis would force reclassification into the subcritical column of the discrimination table and downgrade "excluding pure negative feedback" to "excluding supercritical NF". The same experiment adjudicates whether Lemma 1's subcritical escape hatch is realized by nature—the shortest path from re-analyzing the literature to prediction-driven experiments.

**The gauge principle.** The audit's structural consequence, introduced in §2, deserves emphasis. Because observables depend only on dimensionless combinations, the functionally complete description of a circuit lives in dimensionless coordinates; absolute molecular scales are a redundant gauge. Three practices follow. Experimentally, perturbations and cross-system comparisons are meaningful only when stated on dimensionless groups—comparing absolute rate constants across cell types conflates gauge with physics. Evolutionarily, mutation walks in absolute coordinates while selection reads dimensionless ones; the degeneracy group is thus the neutral drift space available to a circuit, which formalizes the observation that ion-channel degeneracy supports resilience [Goaillard & Marder, 2021]. And functionally, the principle rationalizes why biological sensing is so often ratio-based—fold-change detection (the biological Weber law), morphogen-gradient scaling by tissue size, and the kinase/phosphatase activity ratio of §3: a cell has no external calibration standard, so evolution can only have built its sensors on dimensionless quantities. Counting, the digital limit of §5, is the limiting case in which the observable is dimensionless by construction.

**The cost of going digital.** If counting is calibration-free, why are not all circuits digital? Going digital has costs: an excitable system must be maintained in a near-critical, non-equilibrium state (continuous energy expenditure), and counting at low copy numbers is Poisson-noise-limited. Analog grading is more economical in the shallow regime (few resolvable levels), counting wins in the deep regime, with a crossover at roughly 3–4 resolvable levels. This trade-off rationalizes the observed distribution of regimes: p53, facing catastrophic events (DNA damage) that demand high-fidelity decisions, counts; metabolic sensing that requires continuous fine-tuning of fluxes stays analog. A quantitative energy-versus-resolution framework will be developed elsewhere.

**Reverse predictions: laws from the machine-side audit flow back to cellular circuits.** The present audit is the cellular line of a two-line program; a companion machine line executed the same audit on a different object—artificial perception networks evolved under controlled perturbation regimes, with pre-registered verdicts tracking their coding choices (companion study, in preparation). That line produced three verdict-grade laws, each stating that selection prices invariance. Because the two lines share the same economics—calibration-freeness is purchased, not assumed—the machine-side laws should export testable predictions for cellular circuits in reverse. Each of the three below carries an explicit falsification condition. They are not results of the present paper; they are extrapolation commitments of its methodology, frozen here in advance of testing, and adjudicated either way.

**Prediction 1 (cheapest sufficient invariance → exact price matching).** Across all 54/54 evolutionary arms, the machine line observed that evolution purchases the cheapest invariance just sufficient for the constraint in its own coordinate system—never a stronger one. Cellular translation: a pathway's coding regime should exactly match the pricing of its perturbation environment—counting where gain is uncalibratable (the main line of this paper), ratios where common-mode disturbances dominate (fold-change and ratiometric schemes), analog grading where a stable milieu makes calibration cheap. Test: a cross-pathway census pairing fluctuation spectra with coding regimes. Falsifier: systematic "over-insurance"—pathways maintaining invariance machinery far beyond what their environment requires (e.g., an energy-hungry excitable counting architecture in a gain-stable environment) or, conversely, cheap analog schemes repeatedly found where calibration-free transmission is required, at no cost. **The first cell-side blind adjudication (P2, v0.10) has now executed this prediction's price-side operationalization and ruled it wrong**: on the Msn2 nine-promoter × four-dose single-cell raw trajectories [Hansen & Zechner, 2021], the prediction was that size tercile stratification would significantly alter dose decoding by the molecule-number channel (pre-registered line Δ ≥ 0.03)—the measured stratification penalty was ≈ 0 in all 21/21 units (Δ ∈ [−0.049, +0.029], bootstrap CIs centered on 0), so "size heterogeneity must be priced" carried zero price everywhere; on the same data the calibration-free event-time channel was completely insensitive to stratification (|Δ| ≤ 0.01, reaching line in six of seven promoters, and 7/7 in the k = 2/4 robustness arms), a first blind hit for the survivor class. The clause contracts accordingly: the cell-side redemption of invariance pricing does not run through the size-gain axis; if it exists at all, it must be re-operationalized on an alternative gain axis (e.g., reporter-protein gain calibration) and re-tested.

**Prediction 2 (estimator ordering → window-structure pricing of adaptors versus setpoints).** The machine line's ordering law: estimators are ordered by disturbance-type-dependent relative prices, and an estimator's window structure is itself a pricing variable—frozen-window estimators (fixed reference) are cheap under content noise, whereas batch-window estimators (reference re-estimated from the data batch) pay a content-noise price but purchase robustness to reference drift. Cellular translation: sensory adaptation systems are batch windows—bacterial chemotaxis achieves perfect adaptation that is extraordinarily robust to internal parameter drift (the insensitivity of the methylation integral-feedback loop to protein-concentration fluctuations [Barkai & Leibler, 1997; Yi et al., 2000])—and its price should appear as sensitivity to fluctuations of input statistics (content noise); homeostatic setpoints (integral-feedback homeostasis [Ma et al., 2009]) are frozen windows and should show the opposite price profile. Test: independently manipulate input content noise and internal parameter drift in the same adaptation circuit and measure the two-dimensional profile of adaptation precision. Falsifier: the two sensitivities do not decouple, or the profile directions run opposite to prediction.

**Prediction 3 (two-factor law → code switching by type, not severity).** Machine line: the surviving statistic is determined by two factors—disturbance type and circuit function—with severity nearly irrelevant (regimes unchanged between severity levels 3 and 5). Cellular translation: a circuit's coding regime should be insensitive to a ramp of noise severity (up to functional breakdown) but switch codes when the noise type is swapped at matched severity—retaining counting under multiplicative gain noise, shifting to differencing (DC-elimination) machinery under equally severe additive baseline noise. Test: direct synthetic-biology assay—subject the same synthetic pulse circuit to multiplicative gain noise and additive baseline noise matched in severity, and observe whether the coding regime reorganizes only under the type swap. This is the cheapest and most directly falsifiable of the three.

These three predictions advance the discussion from what the audit has explained to what the audit has prepaid. All three may fail, and failure adjudicates equally: if the coding choices of cellular circuits do not obey the cross-domain economics of invariance, then the digital-limit principle is a cellular special case, and the question posed by this paper's title must be answered domain by domain. Either way, the companion line's next audit will redeem these three predictions as pre-registered items, one by one.

**Blind adjudication of the free statistics (P3): the empirical boundary of sufficiency.** Complementing the price-side test of Prediction 1, a second blind adjudication (v0.10, script 45) tested the **sufficiency** direction of the survivor class directly: on mammalian NF-κB spatial-gradient single-cell raw trajectories (microfluidic source–sink co-culture, per-cell distance from the source [Son et al., 2022]), only per-cell self-referenced free statistics—the event time of first crossing of the cell's own pre-stimulus baseline μ₀ + 3σ₀, and the above-threshold duration—were admitted, with any cross-cell calibration, batch correction, or distance compensation explicitly forbidden, to decode three axes; the decision lines were derived from the null noise floor and frozen before unblinding. Executed verbatim per the frozen decision table: **spatial ordinality hit**—adjacent-band event-time decoding across near/mid/far distance terciles attained AUC = 0.907 and 0.819 (margin 0.55; both bootstrap 95% CIs far from the line); **dose ordinality intermediate**—10↔30 ng/ml holds (0.655 ≥ 0.60) but 30↔100 does not (0.518; response rates already saturated at 0.95→0.99, event times hitting the floor set by 6-min sampling and cascade latency, while the paid peak-amplitude statistic still decodes the same contrast at AUC 0.761); **stimulus-duration axis falsified**—threshold-duration decoding of 15↔30 and 30↔60 min attained AUC = 0.591/0.521, both below the 0.60 line (qualitatively consistent with the duration axis's diagonal indistinguishability in the original paper's single-cell classification heatmap [Son et al., 2022]). The clause contracts accordingly: **the sufficiency of the free statistics is bounded by "unsaturated ordinal axes"**—spatial and low-dose ordinality lie inside it; the high-dose saturation regime and the stimulus-duration axis do not. This boundary complements the theorem layer of §5: the theorems give the necessity and noise bounds of the survivor class (ordinal/event-time functionals); the blind adjudications give its sufficiency range on concrete systems—only together do they constitute the complete "necessary and sufficient" statement.

**Limitations.** Four bound the present claims. First, our information statements concern steady-state and single-readout channels; trajectory-level information can exceed snapshot capacity [Micali & Endres, 2019], and the pulse encoder itself is read over time—the digital-limit principle bounds robustly transferable information per unit of calibration effort, not information in principle. Second, two proofs are representative rather than fully general: Lemma 2 is stated for time-scale-separated systems with fast-subsystem folds, and Lemma 1's Hopf-onset argument is genericity-based, with the subcritical exception handled by the additional signatures rather than excluded a priori. Third, a full five-step audit (including the Fisher-barrier step) is currently completed only for the PdPC and p53 (p53's step 3 was completed in v0.9.3—SI S7, script 13: the exact zero direction of the Fisher spectrum equals the degeneracy-group orbit tangent, structural parameters attain the Cramér–Rao bound, and all three predictions of the conditional theorem are verified numerically); the three systems added in §4 (NF-κB, ERK–MAPK, Msn2-type) validate discrimination, but their audit steps 2–3 remain to be executed system by system. Fourth, the experimental placements on the discrimination diagram vary in precision: p53-DSB/UV and ERK are now source-digitized (Methods), the NF-κB α is taken from the source paper's own quantitative statement, and its ν is read from an SI figure (±0.3 oscillations); placements rest on example traces and reported statistics, and a rigorous population-level audit requires the raw single-cell trajectory sets (registered in the data-availability statement); from v0.10 this registration is partially discharged—the raw-trajectory audits of the Msn2 and NF-κB spatial-gradient systems have landed as this section's two blind adjudications, while the discrimination-diagram placements of the p53/ERK/Tay-NF-κB systems remain bounded by source-figure precision.

**Outlook.** The onset-bifurcation classification of encoding has a thirty-year-old precedent in a neighboring field: neuronal excitability is classified by the same bifurcations (Type I/SNIC versus Type II/Hopf [Rinzel & Ermentrout, 1989]), with the analog signature living in the frequency rather than the amplitude observable. This suggests a general duality—encoding signature as a function of (bifurcation type × observable)—unifying molecular and electrical pulse codes, and points to the degeneracy-group structure of conductance-based neuronal models as the next audit object. The first audit of that object is now complete at textbook Morris–Lecar working points (SI, S2.6 instance table): at Type I/SNIC onset the frequency channel grades as a square-root law while the amplitude channel saturates, and under synaptic-gain drift spanning 0.3×–3× an event-time readout degrades by 0.000% while an amplitude readout drifts by more than 70% on average—gain scale dies, event timing survives. The amplitude leg of the duality, however, fails at subcritical Type II/Hopf onset, where the immediate large-amplitude limit cycle leaves both channels ungraded near onset: the duality's domain is bounded by bifurcation criticality and by the chosen observable. More broadly, if the digital-limit conjecture holds, it is a design principle: synthetic circuits that must transmit dose under unresolvable parameter uncertainty should be built to count. The same principle may rationalize, and perhaps guide, robustness choices wherever analog components suffer mismatch—including engineered ones.

## Methods

**Steady states and degeneracy verification (PdPC).** The Goldbeter–Koshland steady state was solved from ξ = u/(1−u)·(κ+1−u)/(κ+u) by brentq (xtol = 10⁻¹⁴); the κ → 0 limit was evaluated analytically (unit step at ξ = 1). Degeneracy-group actions were applied parametrically (rate rescaling; (K_m, S_T) co-scaling; mixed), and curve deviations were evaluated on a 200-point logarithmic titration grid; invariance held to machine precision (max |Δu| = 0).

**Fisher analysis.** The Fisher information matrix was computed from analytic response Jacobians of u(ξ; ln c, ln κ) and of the absolute-parameter block at observation noise σ = 0.03 (Western-blot scale). Confidence widths for ln κ at deep saturation were obtained by Monte Carlo refitting (n = 400 synthetic curves per κ; 95% CI from percentile).

**Mutual information.** The steady-state channel was discretized: input ensemble log-normal in ξ (σ_ξ = 0.3 narrow, 1.5 wide), Gaussian output noise σ = 0.03, adaptive grids over (x, u). Reported values converged across 300/600/1200-point grids to ±0.02 bits.

**Bifurcation analysis (NF family).** For the three-variable model ṗ = s − δ_p p − γmp/(K+p), ẋ = ap − δ_x x, ṁ = bx − δ_m m (δ_p = 0.02, γ = 1.5, K = 0.15, a = b = 1, δ_x = δ_m = 0.35), the equilibrium branch is closed-form, s(p*) = δ_p p* + γm*p*/(K+p*) with x* = ap*/δ_x, m* = abp*/(δ_xδ_m). Stability was scanned along the branch (6000 points; 3×3 analytic Jacobian); the unique destabilization (s_H = 16.4449) occurs via complex pair ±0.924i. Limit-cycle amplitudes were measured by direct integration (solve_ivp, rtol = 10⁻⁹, atol = 10⁻¹²; 60% transient discarded); A² ∝ (s−s_H) holds with R² = 0.9926.

**Subcritical normal form.** ż = (μ+i)z + |z|²z − |z|⁴z (Re c₁ = +1; quintic stabilization), integrated at rtol = 10⁻¹⁰. Onset/extinction: up-scan from r₀ = 0.05, down-scan from r₀ = 1.2; hysteresis width 0.275 (theory 0.25).

**Morris–Lecar.** Standard Type-II parameters (C = 20; g_Ca = 4.4, g_K = 8, g_L = 2; V_Ca = 120, V_K = −84, V_L = −60; v₁ = −1.2, v₂ = 18, v₃ = 2, v₄ = 30; φ = 0.04). Drive I in the voltage equation; spike amplitude = mean peak − window minimum over the stationary window (40% transient discarded).

**Stochastic pulse population.** Excitable reduction (FitzHugh–Nagumo form, a = 0.7, b = 0.8, ε = 0.08, speed factor 20) with damage input I(D,t) = (0.40+0.09D)·t/(t+0.5)·exp(−t/20), integrated by Euler–Maruyama (dt = 0.001; dynamical noise σ = 0.10; extrinsic cell-to-cell variation: lognormal gain, σ = 0.22, and onset-time jitter, SD = 0.18). 200 cells per dose; pulses: prominence ≥ 1.0, minimum spacing 1.0 a.u. The time unit was calibrated by median inter-pulse interval = 5.5 h.

**Counting law.** Fit N vs ln D₀ over the sustained-counting window (N ≥ 2); self-consistency checked by comparing the period inferred from the slope (τ_r/slope) against the directly measured inter-pulse interval.

**NF-κB oscillator audit.** The Krishna–Jensen–Sneppen three-variable model: dN_n/dt = A(1−N_n)/(ε+I) − B·I·N_n/(δ+N_n), dI_m/dt = N_n² − I_m, dI/dt = I_m − C(1−N_n)I/(ε+I); soft-mode parameters A = 0.007, B = 3, ε = 2×10⁻⁵, δ = 0.005, with IKK strength C as the dose axis. Stability was scanned along the equilibrium branch with the analytic Jacobian to locate the unique loss of stability (C_H = 2.62×10⁻³, complex pair ±0.634i); limit-cycle amplitudes were measured by LSODA integration with transients discarded; linearity of A² ∝ (C−C_H) gives R² = 0.9986; period invariance is the range/mean ratio over the window; hysteresis was assessed by comparing up- and down-sweep endpoints (width zero). A full dose audit of the paper's standard spiky parameter set (B = 954.5, δ = 0.029) was also performed (script 9): equilibrium uniqueness was confirmed by a dense-grid sign scan with brentq; the Hopf boundaries were located precisely by the leading-eigenvalue crossing (C_H = 5.89×10⁻³, ±4.295i; 0.16741, ±17.19i); the limit-cycle branch was traced by a 110-point two-way parameter sweep (slow ramp; LSODA, rtol = 10⁻⁸; maximum up/down amplitude difference 2×10⁻³ → zero hysteresis within numerical precision, single branch); bistability was probed at 8 values of C × 2 initial-condition classes (equilibrium neighborhood / spiky-cycle state), with no coexistence found; a 17-point fine scan of the low onset resolves the continuous Hopf growth from the canard-like near-vertical segment (C ≈ 0.006); α is reported by window (rising limb [0.009, 0.032]: +0.549; canard-inclusive: +0.844; falling limb [0.05, 0.15]: −0.438); spikiness is the fraction of time above half-peak (10.8%). (A limit-cycle folding tentatively observed during early exploration was rejected by the two-way sweep and dual-initial-condition tests; the erratum record is in SI S4.)

**PdPC positive-feedback switch.** Fixed-point equation u = F(ξ(1+g·u)) with F the inverse GK steady state (brentq, xtol = 10⁻¹⁴); equivalently ξ(u) = M(u)/(1+g·u); the main case uses κ = 0.05 (baseline n_H = 11). The critical gain g* = 0.443 was located numerically from the fold condition; apparent Hill coefficients were fitted at half-saturation; hysteresis widths are the dex separations of the up/down fold points; the four degeneracy-group actions were applied pointwise in both the one-root and three-root regions, giving max|Δu| = 0.

**Asymmetric Michaelis constants (script 10).** The asymmetric GK steady state ξ = u/(1−u)·(κ1+1−u)/(κ2+u) (κ_i = K_m,i/S_T) was solved by brentq (xtol = 10⁻¹⁴); degeneracy-group actions were verified pointwise on a 200-point logarithmic titration grid; the Hill coefficient is the logit slope at u = 1/2 (n_H = 4ξ/(dξ/du), checked against the closed form 4/(4−1/(κ1+½)−1/(κ2+½))); Fisher blocks were constructed as in the symmetric case (σ = 0.03, 20-point/3-decade design), shape block (ln c, ln κ1, ln κ2) versus absolute block (ln c, ln K_m,1, ln K_m,2, ln S_T).

**Msn2-type frequency encoding.** The FHN excitable system under constant sustained drive (parameters as in the stochastic-population section; LSODA, rtol = 10⁻⁹). The activity window [0.324, 1.426] au was determined by an oscillation-existence scan; frequency was taken as the inverse steady-state inter-pulse interval on the rising branch, with ν_f the least-squares slope of f vs I₀; a fine onset scan resolves the continuous Hopf square-root from the canard explosion (I_expl = 0.3340); the hysteresis width is the difference of up/down window endpoints (0.0095, subcritical).

**Experimental placement (v0.9 source-figure / source-text quantification).** p53: dose ranges 0.1–10 Gy [Lahav 2004] and 25–400 ng ml⁻¹ NCS [Mönke 2017] (reported statistics); source-figure digitization of [Batchelor 2011] Fig. 1G/H (color-segmentation bar-top extraction with visual axis calibration; NCS 100/200/400 ng ml⁻¹ and UV 2–10 J m⁻²; α as the log–log least-squares slope, with uncertainties re-estimated by bootstrap resampling (×20000) of the digitized bar tops, script 17, SI S10): α_NCS = −0.001 ± 0.113 (95% CI [−0.23, 0.22]), α_UV (2–8 J m⁻²) = +0.981 ± 0.114 (95% CI [0.78, 1.22]); duration exponents NCS −0.21, UV +0.79. The channel-level identifiability audit of the p53 pulse generator (Fisher stratification over the pulse-statistic channels; moment-hierarchy separation; sensing-stage signature; noise-colour discriminator; counting-channel capacity) is deposited as script 16 (SI S9); the hysteresis-experiment protocol design is in SI S11. ERK: digitization of [Albeck 2013] Fig. 3A single-cell EKAR traces (MCF-10A; 0/10/50/200 pg ml⁻¹; 48–72 hr window) and Fig. S4A (184A1; 0/100/500 pg ml⁻¹; three traces per dose) (blue-channel segmentation with per-panel tick calibration; EKAR uncertainty ±0.03); pulse counting by peak detection with prominence > 0.15 EKAR; α_amp as the log–log slope of pulse height vs dose (10→200 pg ml⁻¹), ν as the log–log slope of pulse count vs dose (10→50 pg ml⁻¹, high-dose saturation noted separately). NF-κB: α from the source paper's own quantitative statement [Tay 2010] (median first-peak nuclear intensity changing four-fold over four orders of magnitude of dose → α = 0.151); ν read from its Supp Fig. 5 (±0.3 oscillations); first-peak-area constancy as stated in the source text. All digitization scripts and intermediate data are deposited as supplementary software.

## Data availability

No new experimental data were generated in this study. Three published single-cell datasets were re-analyzed: p53 (Batchelor et al., 2011, source Fig. 1G/H), NF-κB (Tay et al., 2010, the paper's quantitative statements and Supp. Fig. 5), and ERK (Albeck et al., 2013, Figs. 3A and S4A). Digitized values, calibration notes, and all intermediate data are deposited together with the analysis code (below). The raw single-cell trajectory sets required for a rigorous population-level audit remain with the original authors and are registered as a limitation (§6). From v0.10, two further author-published raw single-cell trajectory sets are used for the pre-registered blind adjudications (re-analysis only): Msn2 (Hansen & Zechner, 2021; Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.2755026; 9 promoters × 30 conditions) and the NF-κB spatial gradient (Son et al., 2022; Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.6858118; dose/duration/distance series); the pre-registration documents, decision-line freeze records, and adjudication logs are deposited together with the code.
